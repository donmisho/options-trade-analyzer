## Relevant Context — Do Not Deviate Without Escalation

Source: insight_engine.md § 3.8 (Verdict bands)
Rule: Verdict bands are a **monotonic, per-strategy** mapping from final adjusted score to a categorical verdict, stored in the strategy's `verdict_band_set`. The engine treats verdict labels as **opaque strings** — `EXECUTE` / `A` / etc. carry no meaning to the engine. Screening uses `EXECUTE/WAIT/PASS`; health grading uses `A/B/C/D/F`; both come from the **same Phase-7 band lookup**, selected by the candidate's `candidate_type`.

Source: insight_engine.md § 3.8 (audit callout — verbatim target)
Fact: The audit explicitly names this defect — the current OTA implementation hardcodes universal `EXECUTE/WAIT/PASS` thresholds (70/50) in `evaluation_routes.py`, a violation of the tables-as-source principle. This story removes that.

Source: insight_engine.md § 4.1 / pipeline (Phase 7)
Rule: Phase 7 = Verdict band lookup — the final score is mapped to a verdict via the strategy's bands. Fixed pipeline order (gates → scoring → adjustments → verdict); a verdict is never assigned before adjustments apply.

Source: insight_engine.md § 2 (Architecture principle — single path)
Rule: "There is no second evaluation path. Duplicative rule logic across consumers is a design defect." Do not introduce or preserve an app-layer verdict computation parallel to the engine's band lookup.

Source: insight_engine.md § 6.6 (load-time validation)
Rule: A non-monotonic `verdict_band_set` is a hard load failure. Runtime band lookup may assume the loaded config is monotonic (it was validated at load); it must NOT re-implement monotonicity enforcement in the route.

Source: CLAUDE.md § House Style — Provider/rule literals
Rule: Never hardcode rule content (thresholds, band numbers) in route/app code. Rule content lives in the tables.
